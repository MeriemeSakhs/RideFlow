Driver SMS Consent — Verbal Opt-In Script

Last updated: October 7, 2026

Drivers are not required to join RideFlow's text-messaging program. When a company's manager or dispatcher adds a driver to their roster, they offer the driver the option to receive ride-assignment text messages by reading the script below out loud, in person or by phone. This page documents the exact script used, as publicly reviewable evidence of how consent is captured.

The Script

Hi [Driver Name], I'd like to ask if you want to receive text messages from RideFlow about your ride assignments. If you say yes, we'll text you at this number whenever you're offered a new ride, with a link to confirm or decline it. Message frequency varies based on ride activity, and message and data rates may apply. You can reply HELP at any time for help, or STOP at any time to stop these messages. This is optional — you don't have to say yes to be added as a driver. Before I mark your answer, I also want to let you know you can review RideFlow's Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy at meriemesakhs.github.io/RideFlow/terms/ and meriemesakhs.github.io/RideFlow/privacy/, which explain that your number is never sold or shared with anyone else for marketing. Do you agree to receive these text messages, and have you had a chance to review those terms?

What Happens After

If the driver agrees, the manager checks the "the driver has agreed to receive ride assignment text messages from RideFlow at this phone number" box on the Add/Manage Driver screen in RideFlow, which records the driver's consent together with the date and time it was given. If the driver does not agree, the box is left unchecked and no messages are ever sent to that driver.

A driver can withdraw consent at any time, either by replying STOP to a RideFlow text message, or by asking their manager to update their consent status in RideFlow — which immediately stops any further messages to that number.
